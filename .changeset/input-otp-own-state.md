InputOTP's slots now take their invalid and disabled looks only from InputOTP's own `<input>`. Inside an element marked `group`, such as a `<div className="group">` or a `<fieldset className="group">`, and inside the library's `AccordionItem` and `Toast`, which are marked `group`, the slots took the look of any other control there. An invalid Input, DateField, TimeField or another InputOTP gave them the `otl-danger-300` border, and the active slot the `fr-danger` ring. A disabled Button, Checkbox or Input, or a `<select>` with a disabled placeholder `<option>`, gave them the `bg-washed` fill and `txt-black-500` text. A control among InputOTP's children, such as a disabled Resend button, did the same. All the while InputOTP's own input stayed valid and enabled, and assistive technology announced it so. Now the slots have the `otl-danger-300` border exactly when the input has `aria-invalid="true"`, from `invalid` or an `aria-invalid` you pass, and the disabled look exactly when the input is `:disabled`, from `disabled` or a `<fieldset disabled>`. InputOTP's own looks don't change. Its container's class is now `group/input-otp` instead of `group`, so a bare `group-*` class you put on InputOTP's children or on `InputOTPSlot` no longer keys off the container: pass `containerClassName="group"`, or use the `/input-otp` form, such as `group-hover/input-otp:`. `full.css` in `@amerfathullah/myds-style` carries the new classes.
